# What the October reruns changed

The question addressed by the October 28 report is whether replacing selected executions or changing a scoring rule altered the benchmark conclusions. The report contains 77 replacement records, including the answer or output score before and after each replacement. The [run ledger](s2_reruns/dataset/report_runs.csv) identifies the page, question, model, condition, replicate, reason, original and replacement values, and links to evidence. The [verification table](s2_reruns/dataset/verified_replacements.csv) joins each entry to its October result. The [question index](fig1_performance/dataset/question_index.csv) covers all 160 questions and distinguishes a published answer from an output evaluator.

## What changed when the selected runs were repeated?

| CompBioBench | 28 / 27 | 22 of 55 answer strings changed. DeepSeek gained two total official points across three submissions in each condition. Luna lost two in each. The four-model official means remained 87.00% for Galaxy and 86.75% for custom code. |
| IWC | 1 / 2 | All three replacements evaluated DeepSeek peptide–protein matches. Galaxy R2 went from 100.00% to 98.23% F1. Custom-code R1 went from 100.00% to 98.23%, and R2 from 99.28% to 90.26%. Across all 30 DeepSeek runs per condition, mean agreement moved from 99.94% to 99.88% and from 96.28% to 95.92%, respectively. |

Fourteen BixBench originals accessed earlier answers or same-question traces, including three direct copies on bix-30-q3. For five others, exposure to that question’s answer was unconfirmed. The count for bix-30-q3 is now 10/12 accepted Galaxy answers and 4/12 custom-code answers. For bix-45-q1 the October rule accepts the older and newer PhyKIT results within 5% relative tolerance. All 24 primary answers pass the revised numerical rule. A historical description of all Galaxy runs failing this item refers to a retired key. ## How did agents complete work in Galaxy?

An earlier BixBench campaign in May 2026 had no UDTs. Installed Galaxy tools alone solved 42 of its 50 tasks, one run per task. The published card states that this shows at least 42 tasks are solvable without a UDT, not that the other eight need one. It does not name the model. [Archived card](sources/bixbench_index.html) · [Table](s1_execution/dataset/no_udt_baseline.csv)

The current exports link a Galaxy history for all 1,920 primary Galaxy assignments. The BixBench route field records UDT use in 234/600 runs. In CompBioBench, 844/1,200 used a UDT, including 450 that also ran standard tools. The BixBench export has a null route for 366 runs. The published model summaries establish no recorded UDT in those runs but do not distinguish standard-only from no analysis job. The IWC export describes workflow steps and exposes run and reference histories; it has no equivalent complete UDT classification. [Run histories](s1_execution/dataset/run_histories.csv) · [Execution routes](s1_execution/dataset/source_data.csv)

The RNA-seq example follows a specific output through the workbench. In IWC 002, GPT-5.5 Galaxy R1 ran the DESeq2 wrapper 2.11.40.8+galaxy4 on prefiltered counts and produced history dataset 16. The public job state was “ok.” All 2,526 output genes occur in the 7,127-gene raw reference table. The two tables each report 13 adjusted P values below 0.05, with 12 genes in common. The full benchmark score of 1.0 uses a fixed prefiltered evaluation universe. Comparing the two raw history outputs answers a different question and demonstrates why the output and its reference universe both belong in a validation record. ## Which problems should Galaxy address?

Of 133 incorrect BixBench runs, 84 had a primary label for task or scoring ambiguity (77 task-specification and seven scoring-rule labels), 27 for missing validation with an assigned cause, six for the platform or agent harness (three each, all under Galaxy) and five for model knowledge. Four further validation audits marked their cause unresolved and seven runs await review. For each of the five knowledge errors, the same model passed the same task under the same policy in another replicate. [Failure ledger with policy split](fig2_interface/dataset/bixbench_current_failures.csv)

| Observation | Development implication | How to test it |
|---|---|---|
| In the July 6 GPT-5.5 batch, Galaxy runs took 2.66× as many agent turns as custom-code runs, and each turn carried 2.20× as much cached context. Cached input was 94.8% of Galaxy tokens and output 0.7%. Platform and API commands numbered 1,851 against 154. | Cost is turns × context. Return compact results so each turn stays small, and wait on long jobs inside one call instead of polling. These are the changes made in the October batch. | Report turns, cached input per turn and acceptance for each interface version under a fixed model and CLI. [Supplementary Fig. 7](s7_token_drivers/README.md) |
| The historical audit recorded 1,121 tool-schema failures involving history context. In the same archive, tools inspected but never run accounted for 876/1,942 BixBench, 2,980/6,357 CompBioBench and 399/730 IWC inspections. | Carry the active history into schema lookup and expose dataset-compatible inputs. A lookup can legitimately end without execution. | Compare failed lookups and subsequent successful submissions per task under matched prompts. |
| The historical audit counted 1,339 job errors without a diagnostic. It also distinguished missing dependencies, invalid identifiers, nested parameter structures and runtime errors with stderr. | Return the job ID, failing stage and available diagnostic; identify invalid parameters before submission. | Measure recoveries after a first failure and the number of repeat submissions, alongside final output agreement. |
| In the current BixBench failures, 72/77 specification labels occur in groups where all three replicates failed. In contrast, 16/31 missing-validation labels occur with two other replicates correct. | Expose the tool version and resolved scientific parameters, and let agents compare a produced output with explicit task checks. Seven replacement failures still need trace adjudication. | Test validation on withheld tasks, including negative-strand coordinates and version-sensitive statistics. |
| Interface and waiting changes reduced model requests and tokens in the Sol batch. | Return compact job summaries while retaining full records for inspection; allow a long-running call to finish before asking for status again. | Hold CLI, model, task, scoring rule and accounting coverage fixed in a prospective rerun. |

The failed-request evidence precedes the October integrity reruns. Request counts can include several events in one successful run. The current BixBench cause census retains 122 run-level and four task-level labels. Its seven uncovered replacement failures are listed individually. Proposed changes above are development hypotheses. Evidence of a fix in the agent interface does not establish a change in Galaxy core, deployment of a new Tool Shed wrapper, or benefit to human users. ## What was independently checked?

For the replacement on compute-gccontent-promoter-q1, the UCSC GENCODE V49 record identifies ENST00000269305.9 on the negative strand and places its transcription start at chr17:7,687,490. With 500 bases upstream and 100 downstream, the one-based closed interval spans 7,687,391–7,687,990. We counted 292 G/C bases in the 600-base hg38 sequence, giving 0.49 after rounding. That independently reproduces the corrected answer. We also compared the two IWC yeast tables directly and independently applied the published numerical rule to every primary PhyKIT answer. The remaining replacement checks establish agreement between the report and public exports; the hidden CompBioBench key and controlled-access traces limit further biological or causal verification. ## How much did token use fall?

On GPT-5.6 Sol BixBench the mean fell from 122.41 million to 54.35 million per 50 tasks, a 55.6% reduction; custom code averaged 35.51 million. Compact replies alone were observed in a single intermediate replicate (85.93 million). Relative to that batch, the three longer-wait replicates averaged 78.3 versus 481 wait calls and 1,332.3 versus 1,799 model requests. The CLI changed from 0.146.0 to 0.156.0 between the original and optimized Galaxy campaigns. These are sequential batch comparisons. The early 20× and 8× ratios are approximate author reports. [Optimization source data](fig2_interface/dataset/source_data.csv) · [Historical stages](s6_token_usage/dataset/historical_rounds.csv)

The optimization release credits 137/150. The three optimized bix-45-q1 answers all equal the newer accepted value and were originally marked incorrect. Applying only the revised PhyKIT rule yields 140/150 as a **sensitivity calculation**. The currently published standard Sol Galaxy runs score 139/150. Keep the sensitivity calculation separate from the optimized batch’s published result. [Run-level rescoring](fig2_interface/dataset/optimization_key_sensitivity.csv)

The July 6 batch also ran both policies without skill guidance. That guidance included telling agents to save bulky Galaxy responses to files. Without it, Galaxy used 502.8 million rather than 446.7 million tokens over 150 runs, and accepted 126 rather than 139 answers. [Skills comparison](s7_token_drivers/dataset/source_data.csv)

Across the four models in the October main runs, Galaxy/custom-code total-token ratios are 4.07 (BixBench), 2.48 (CompBioBench) and 0.96 (IWC). The earlier note’s 4.2, 2.7 and 0.92 were calculated from the October 12 edition. CompBioBench October totals cover 1,199/1,200 Galaxy and 1,200/1,200 custom-code runs. The reported values sum observed usage and leave the missing run unimputed. [Current token counts and coverage](s6_token_usage/dataset/replicate_usage.csv)

## Which claims can enter the main text?

Removing the seven BixBench questions implicated by historical primary specification or scorer labels leaves 496/516 accepted in each condition. This is a retrospectively selected subset. The sign of the BixBench difference depends on one question:
- grading only bix-45-q1 by its pre-October status gives 525 Galaxy and 526 custom-code acceptances;
- pre-review grades for every question give 523 and 521;
- excluding bix-45-q1 gives 525 and 518 of 588.

The pooled 95% intervals use 200,000 cluster resamples: −1.5 to 4.2 points for BixBench and 0.8 to 7.3 for IWC. Most of the IWC difference comes from workflows 005, 010 and 007. [Sensitivity analyses](s3_repeatability/dataset/sensitivity.csv) · [Pooled intervals](fig1_performance/dataset/source_data.csv)

Replacement runs extend beyond the report. The exports identify 78 BixBench Galaxy runs from recovery or repeat batches: 72 GPT-5.6 Luna, 2 GPT-5.6 Sol and 4 DeepSeek. They also mark 52 CompBioBench runs as reruns that the report does not cover, 34 of them GPT-5.5 Galaxy. Their history names date these to 23 September–1 October. The frozen sources do not say why these runs were repeated, so the manuscript discloses them and the authors must give the reason. [Batch of every run](s2_reruns/dataset/run_batches.csv) The IWC sensitivity table leaves one of the ten workflows out at a time and reports both condition means and their difference. [Sensitivity analyses](s3_repeatability/dataset/sensitivity.csv)

Main performance comparisons use the October reviewed BixBench passes, official CompBioBench submission means and IWC continuous scores. CompBioBench’s reconstructed item key matches 1,039/1,200 Galaxy and 1,059/1,200 custom-code outputs. That item-level contrast points in the opposite direction from the official submission contrast and belongs only in error and repeatability diagnostics. For IWC, 98 of 240 outputs have a score below 1; explanations include numerical precision and permitted method differences as well as incorrect outputs. A cutoff of 0.99 is used only for the replicate diagnostic. A vote over correctness uses the scoring key; an operational answer vote needs a rule for different strings and ties. Both tables are supplied.
